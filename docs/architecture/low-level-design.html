ShowMe — Low-Level Design

How ShowMe answers product questions with text, pictures, and video — and why (with one deliberate, labeled MVP exception) it never shows you something it hasn’t verified.

Version 0.7 Date 2026-08-20 Status Draft for implementation review Scope P0 — Text, Image & Video answers Full contract: low-level-design.md
1

What this system does (and refuses to do)

Maps to LLD §1 — purpose, scope, and document hierarchy.

In plain words ShowMe is like a very careful product expert. You ask a question about a product — “How do I fold this stroller?” — and it answers with text, a labeled photo, step cards, or a video. The twist: it is only allowed to say or show things it has already proven from real sources (the manual, the maker’s website, official videos). If it can’t prove something, it says so instead of guessing.

In scope for P0 (the first version)

  • Knowing exactly which product and variant you mean.
  • Verified factual text answers, exact product photos with callouts, illustrated step sequences.
  • Retrieved verified video segments, plus purpose-built video that is prepared and checked offline first.
  • Follow-up questions, compound questions (“can I… and where… and show me how”), mixed-format answers.
  • Assisted human review of content, and full traceability: every answer can be traced back to its sources.
  • Honest partial answers: “here’s what we know; the rest is being prepared / not supported / needs an expert.”

Deliberately not in P0

  • Generating a how-to video live, while you wait. (Generation itself always happens offline. MVP exception, LLD §1.3: during the MVP, the freshly generated video is shown to the asker as soon as it exists — clearly labeled as unverified — and verification runs after serving instead of before. C3 safety-critical procedures are excluded, and the exception sunsets once the automated VLM check runs inline.)
  • Showing invented, “plausible-looking” motion as if it were a real instruction.
  • Exact 3D answers: fit, clearance, collision, physics simulation. (Reserved for a later phase — §21.)

Document hierarchy: product requirements say what ShowMe must do; system-architecture.md and showme-system-architecture.md give the high-level picture; this LLD defines the component boundaries, contracts, states, and failure behavior. Where documents disagree, the LLD governs.

Eight words you need first

Every diagram below uses these terms.

Source
An original artifact we ingested: a manual PDF, an official product page, a manufacturer video. Stored forever, never edited.
Claim
One small proven fact card extracted from a source: “max child weight = 50 lb”, “step 2: squeeze the handle lever”. Each claim points at the exact page/region/timestamp it came from.
Asset
A piece of media we can show: a photo with a callout, a step-card sequence, a video clip. Every asset is bound to the claims it demonstrates.
Candidate vs. Published
Anything newly extracted or newly generated is a candidate — invisible to users. Only after verification does it become published and servable. (MVP-only: a generated video candidate may be served in a labeled CANDIDATE_PROVISIONAL state — see §1.3 exception.)
Consequence level (C0–C3)
How much harm a wrong answer could cause. C0/C1 ≈ harmless-to-annoying (color, warranty length). C2 ≈ could waste money or damage the product. C3 ≈ could hurt a person (car-seat installation). Higher level ⇒ stricter rules and human approval.
Answer IR
“Intermediate Representation” — the answer as structured, typed data (operators, values, claim IDs) before it is turned into sentences. Machines can check it; prose can’t sneak facts in.
Answer Manifest
The permanent receipt for every delivered answer: exactly which claim versions, asset versions, and policy versions produced it. Makes every answer auditable and correctable later.
Lane
A production line in the Asset Factory. Lanes A–E make different kinds of media (exact photo callout, purpose-built visual, step cards, procedural video, labeled transition).

The ten unbreakable rules

Maps to LLD §1.2 — non-negotiable implementation rules. Every component below exists to enforce one or more of these.

1A source is not a claim

Having the manual in storage proves nothing. A fact only counts after it’s extracted, checked, and published as a claim.

2Generated ≠ published

A freshly generated image or video is just a candidate. Users never see it until verification publishes it. (Suspended for MVP by §1.3: the asker sees the labeled candidate immediately; verification runs afterward.)

3Match before rank

Retrieved media must match the exact product, revision, rights, and freshness before we even ask “is it relevant?”. A similar-looking sibling product never sneaks in.

4Every claim gets its own route

A question is split into its material claims. Each claim is routed and risk-rated separately — one hard part can’t hide behind an easy part.

5No live generated video

A generated procedural video never enters an answer until it has finished offline verification and publication. (Suspended for MVP by §1.3, except C3 safety-critical procedures, which always stay behind verification.)

6Generation presents, never invents

A purpose-built visual may re-express a proven fact in a new medium, but generation can never supply a missing fact or pass off invented motion as product behavior. Illustrative motion only appears in an explicitly labeled non-instructional transition.

7Words come from typed data

Final language is realized from a gated, typed Answer IR — never accepted as free-form model prose about the product.

8Every answer leaves a receipt

Each delivered answer writes a durable, version-pinned Answer Manifest. Privacy erasure is itself a recorded lifecycle event.

9Changes propagate

Every publish, stale, retire, and delete transition flows to everything that depends on it. A corrected manual invalidates the claims and assets built on it.

10Real video first, untouched

Existing video may be returned as-is or trimmed when it completely answers the question. Painting overlays onto source footage is not the primary strategy.

MVP exception (LLD §1.3) For the MVP, rules 2 and 5 are deliberately suspended on the video-miss path: when no published video exists, the freshly generated clip is shown to the asker as soon as generation finishes — before any verification — carrying a non-removable “AI-generated, not yet checked against the manual” label, always alongside the verified written steps. It is stored as CANDIDATE_PROVISIONAL and may be re-served in that labeled state until offline verification completes: pass ⇒ published (label removed); fail ⇒ quarantined and every user who saw it is notified. C3 safety-critical procedures (car-seat installation, harness threading) are excluded. The exception sunsets when the automated VLM check runs inline in the generation job, or at Phase 4 scale — whichever comes first.

The big map: two worlds, one shelf

Maps to LLD §2 — high-level component map, and §4 — initial deployables.

In plain words ShowMe is two worlds. The Online Answer System is fast — it must reply in seconds, so it is only allowed to read from the shelf of already-published facts and media. The Offline Product Factory is slow and careful — it reads manuals, extracts facts, makes media, verifies everything, and stocks the shelf. The two worlds never hand each other raw material directly; everything passes through the published data stores.

How this deploys (without 15 microservices)

The boxes above are logical boundaries. P0 packs them into a few deployables and only splits further for a concrete reason (independent scaling, security isolation, latency isolation, team ownership):

The client itself is not a dumb chat window: it renders claim-level states without hiding partial or unsupported parts, tracks the active product/step/viewpoint, shows deferred-job status honestly (never an infinite spinner), provides accessible media controls (pause, seek, captions, transcript, step navigation), and lets users report wrong answers with the exact answer_id.

Answering a question, step by step

Maps to LLD §3.2 and §5 — the Online Answer System.

In plain words Think of an assembly line with 12 stations. Your question enters at one end; a checked, receipt-backed answer comes out the other. No station may skip ahead, and the second-to-last station — the Delivery Gate — can stop everything.

What each station does

1Front door — API Gateway

Checks who you are, what you may access, request size and rate limits; assigns request_id, trace_id, idempotency key; rejects unsafe file types. Nothing inside runs if this fails.

2Safety scan — Raw Request Policy Scan

Reads the raw question before anything else and answers: which domains does this touch (e.g. child restraints)? What is the minimum risk floor? What identity details are mandatory? What warnings must appear? A planner can’t “forget” a hazard it never got to hide.

3Which product? — Identity Resolver

Pins down product, SKU, revision, market to the confidence the risk floor demands. Explicit selection beats visual similarity; look-alike siblings are never substituted. Unclear → ask the user, don’t guess.

4Split into claims — Claim Planner

Turns “Can I use these headphones with this laptop, where’s the port, and show me how?” into three separate claims (compatibility, part location, procedure) with dependencies. It decides what to check, never what is true.

5Anything missed? — Completeness Validator

An independent double-checker with two layers: deterministic rules (every quantity, negation, condition, and safety phrase must map to a claim) plus a separately-configured semantic pass. Neither can waive the other’s failure; disagreement on a C2/C3 obligation forces a clarification or the stricter route.

6How strict? — Consequence Policy Engine

Deterministic, versioned. effective = max(raw floor, per-claim floor, planner request) — the level can be raised, never lowered. It dictates required validation states, warnings, human-approval needs, and allowed answer modes.

7Route each claim — Route Planner

Picks the best format per claim using the route table below — and defines the honest fallback when the ideal media doesn’t exist yet.

8Fetch the proof — Evidence + Retrieval + Derivation

Evidence Query returns only servable claim versions with exact source bindings. Asset Retrieval filters by identity, lifecycle, rights, and freshness before ranking by relevance. The Deterministic Derivation Service may compute new values — but only with an allow-listed formula over published operands, with units, uncertainty, and the formula version recorded. It never estimates an unknown fact.

9Build typed answer — Answer IR Builder

Assembles per-claim results into structured data: operators, values, certainty, sources, warnings, media bindings. response_state is a closed enum: ANSWERED · PARTIAL · CLARIFICATION · DEFERRED · UNSUPPORTED · ESCALATION · TEMPORARY_FAILURE.

10Delivery Gate

The final inspector. Big enough to get its own section — §6.

11Careful writer — Controlled Realizer

Turns the gated IR into sentences. Prose may improve readability but may not add product claims. High-consequence answers use constrained templates; critical warnings are always in text, never only in audio/motion/color; every video comes with a transcript or equivalent step list. Streaming only ever starts from an already-gated IR.

12Send + receipt — Answer Manifest

The realized response (or its hash) plus every pinned version — claims, assets, policies, planner, gate, schema — is durably written before the user gets the answer. Every answer is auditable forever.

The fast lane (with the same guards)

A simple question (“what’s the max weight?”) may skip the model-based planner and semantic completeness pass — but only when identity is already explicit, the request matches a versioned deterministic intent grammar as a single direct fact, the safety scan says C0/C1 with no mandatory clarification, and a published claim matches exactly. Fast-path answers still pass the policy engine, the Delivery Gate, and the realizer, and their manifests record fast_path: true. Any uncertainty falls back to the full pipeline.

The route table: which format for which claim

Video-first for actions (decision 2026-08-20) “Least complex format first” applies only to pure facts. Anything the user has to do — a physical or on-screen action, however small — targets video as its ideal format, even when a single sentence would technically answer (“plug into the headphone jack” is a video; “how much does it weigh” is not). The text/step equivalent always ships alongside, and evidence requirements are unchanged — this changes what we aim to show, not what we’re allowed to show.
Ask before we generate (LLD §5.16) Vague action questions (“connect Bose headphones to Mac” — which headphones? which Mac? wired or Bluetooth?) trigger one multiple-choice clarification before any generation job is enqueued. Options come from typed catalog data, so a tap binds directly to the generation spec — no guessing, no free-text parsing. If the user doesn’t answer: for C0/C1 the system picks a default deterministically and declares it (“Showing: QC Ultra → MacBook Air M3, Bluetooth — tap to change”), recording the assumption in the manifest; for C2/C3 it never assumes and stays in clarification. Session-known attributes are never re-asked, and clarifying narrows the spec — it never downgrades an action back to text.

The Delivery Gate: the last inspector

Maps to LLD §5.13 — the component that makes “never show unverified content” an enforced property instead of a hope.

In plain words Imagine a security guard at the exit of the factory shop. Every single answer — even a “sorry, could you clarify?” — must pass this guard. The guard doesn’t use judgment or vibes: they run a fixed, versioned checklist against the structured answer. If anything on the checklist fails, the answer either gets repaired, loses its broken part, or is replaced by a safe honest response. There is no side door.

The one batched freshness check

Before running its rules, the gate issues a single logical BatchResolveEligibility request carrying every pinned claim and asset version, the tenant/rights context, and one as_of timestamp. The Lifecycle Eligibility Resolver reads all authoritative registries in parallel and returns lifecycle, applicability, rights, freshness, consequence ceiling, and per-registry watermarks. Rules:

  • Sequential per-item lookups are prohibited on the normal path — one batch, set-oriented reads.
  • A cache may accelerate this only with push-based invalidation, a bounded maximum age, and authoritative fallback. A cache can never turn “unknown” into “eligible”.
  • If the batch check is unavailable or a watermark is too stale, the gate fails closed.

Even the “sorry” is gated

Clarifications, deferred-status updates, and terminal-failure messages pass a reduced gate profile too: mandatory safety warnings present, no unbound product claims, approved templates only — and each one writes a lightweight manifest recording what was asked and why.

“We’re preparing your video” — the deferred path

Maps to LLD §3.3 and §7.3 — retrieval miss, bounded budgets, and fallbacks.

In plain words Sometimes the facts are proven but the perfect video doesn’t exist yet. ShowMe never makes you wait for generation: it answers right now with the best verified thing it has (usually illustrated steps or text) and tells you a video is being prepared. MVP behavior (§1.3): the moment generation finishes (typically 3–7 minutes), you’re shown the clip — clearly labeled as AI-generated and not yet checked — while verification runs afterward; if it later fails, the clip is pulled and you’re notified. Target behavior (post-MVP): the clip stays invisible until it passes verification, and only then do you get the “it’s ready!” notification. Either way, if generation keeps failing the job doesn’t spin forever — it falls back or fails honestly, with an owner and a deadline.
  • No candidate video ever ships straight from the factory. Publication happens only through verification.
  • Idempotency: the job key is a SHA-256 over tenant, exact identity, asset type, pinned claim versions, coverage, consequence, and rights — so a republished claim creates a new job rather than silently reusing output built on superseded facts.
  • Corrections never edit in place: a human may trim, re-label, or fix the spec, but the result is always a new candidate version that re-enters verification.

From manual to fact: the evidence pipeline

Maps to LLD §3.1 and §6 — Source Intake, Vault, processors, extraction, validation.

In plain words Before ShowMe can answer anything about a product, the factory reads that product’s official material and turns it into small, checkable fact cards (claims). Each card remembers exactly where it came from — down to the page region or video timestamp — so any answer can be traced back to its source, and any source correction can find every card it affects.

The auto-publish lane is deliberately tiny

A claim may publish without a human only when all of these hold (LLD §6.5):

  • It’s C0/C1 and its claim type is explicitly allow-listed by a versioned policy.
  • The source is an allow-listed first-party authority (official product page, manufacturer spec table).
  • Product, variant, revision, market, and effective dates match exactly (or are explicitly source-wide).
  • The value has an exact source span and passes a deterministic type/unit/range parser.
  • Two independently configured extraction passes agree — or one agrees with a deterministic table parser. (This checks transcription, not truth; it never resolves a source conflict.)
  • No conflicting source, warning, recall, or unresolved applicability exists.
  • The claim is directly stated — never inferred, calculated, or copied from a sibling product.
  • It is not a compatibility relationship, procedure, safety warning/limit, diagnosis, or a dimension meant for fit.

Everything else — and every C2/C3 claim — goes to human review. Every auto-published claim records the policy version, both extraction results, and reason codes.

Making pictures and videos: Lanes A–E

Maps to LLD §7 — Media Coverage Analyzer, generation specs, and factory lanes.

In plain words The factory has five production lines. Each makes a different kind of media, but they all follow the same law: the media may only show what the published claims already prove. A lane can change how a fact looks, never what is true. And every lane’s output is a locked “candidate” that must survive verification before anyone sees it.
LaneMakesThe one rule that defines it

These are internal production sub-lanes of the three user-facing formats (Text / Image / Video) from the architecture docs — they add no new user-facing modality.

The generation spec, in one breath

Every attempt compiles a provider-neutral, immutable AssetGenerationSpec: identity, bound claim versions, input assets by registry reference (never durable provider URLs), required states, motion constraints per claim, presentation limits, prohibited content (NEW_CONTROLS, OMITTED_REQUIRED_ACTIONS, IDENTITY_CHANGE), the verification policy to face, an execution budget (max attempts / cost / deadline), and the fallback lanes. Adapters implement a fixed contract — GetCapabilities · ValidateSpec · SubmitAttempt · GetAttempt · CancelAttempt · CollectCandidate — and everything a provider returns is treated as untrusted input, with full audit of settings, hashes, timing, and cost.

The factory learns (LLD §7.8, decision 2026-08-20) Every verification verdict — pass and fail — is reusable learning signal, not just a per-attempt retry hint. Each spec pins a versioned recipe_id (pin configuration, prompt template, camera policy, provider + tier); an offline job aggregates verdicts into pass-rate tables per lane and product category, and the winning recipe is promoted as a versioned policy change, never a silent prompt edit. Guard rail: a verifier signal may drive automated promotion only after it’s benchmarked against human labels — otherwise we’d train ourselves to fool the judge. Rejected candidates are kept as a hard-negative library: a forever regression suite for the verifier, and “not like this” exemplars for prompts. Fine-tuning the generator itself is out of P0 — re-evaluated only if the digital-twin path leaves generation owning motion (see digital-twin-phase-plan.md, Decision 9).

What a published asset remembers

An asset record pins its exact identity, its claim bindings with time ranges (this second of video demonstrates step 2), versioned caption/transcript assets, step markers for player navigation, its consequence ceiling, rights, and the verification approvals that let it publish. Step markers are player metadata — not text burned onto source footage. If a video has no meaningful audio, an equivalent verified step sequence may stand in for the transcript, but critical actions must always exist outside motion alone.

Humans in the loop: verification & Content-Ops

Maps to LLD §7.5 and §8 — asset verification layers and the review workbench.

In plain words Machines check the checkable; humans judge the judgeable. Every generated asset passes automated checks first, and anything that instructs a person — or could hurt one — additionally requires a qualified human to approve it before it publishes.

The four verification layers

  1. Deterministic checks — identity, rights, file integrity, duration, required labels, claim/step bindings.
  2. Automated content checks — product consistency, part/control visibility, ordered coverage, state consistency.
  3. Operator review — as required by consequence and instruction policy. Any instruction requires human approval in P0. C2/C3 assets require consequence-appropriate approval even when non-instructional.
  4. Publish transaction — only after all required approvals pass.

The review workbench

Reviewers work queues (claim conflicts, low-confidence identity, C2/C3 approvals, procedure steps, media coverage, generated assets, stale/recall remediation, exhausted jobs). For each item the workbench shows the candidate, the exact product identity, the precise source page/region/timestamp, conflicting sources, the applicable policy, and a hard-failure checklist — with approve / correct / quarantine / reject / stale / retire actions and an immutable review history.

  • A reviewer’s role caps the consequence level they may approve; C3 may require dual or specially qualified review.
  • Reviewers can’t approve outside their tenant/catalog scope.
  • Every decision records reviewer, timestamp, policy version, and rationale.

The life story of a fact (or asset)

Maps to LLD §9.2 and §3.4 — lifecycle states, invalidation, and the one carefully-bounded grace period.

In plain words Every claim and asset lives in exactly one state, and only one state is visible to users: PUBLISHED. If anything about a fact might have changed — the manual was corrected, a recall happened, rights expired — it is immediately pulled from view (quarantined) and reviewed. Content is never edited in place; corrections create new versions.

When a source changes

A change is substantive when it may alter answer truth or permitted use — source bytes, product revision, applicability, authority, recall, correction, rights, safety policy, or verification outcome. Substantive or uncertain ⇒ affected claims and assets are quarantined immediately, removed from retrieval, and their delivery URLs revoked; affected Answer Manifests are identified and, where required, users are notified.

The only softer path is PUBLISHED_REVALIDATING: a C0/C1 item after a non-semantic tooling change (same source hash, identity, rights, policy outcome, and prior verification evidence) may keep serving for a policy-bounded grace period of at most 48 hours while shadow revalidation runs. Pass ⇒ republish; fail or expire ⇒ quarantine. C2/C3 content never uses this state.

User reports

A user report (“wrong product / wrong step / unsafe”) loads the Answer Manifest with its exact versions, is triaged — potentially-unsafe or systemic issues can quarantine content immediately — and then resolves through the same versioned correction → revalidation → republication → invalidation machinery as every other change. Reports never modify published content directly.

Jobs, events, and notifications

Maps to LLD §9 — job states, core events, and the Notification Service.

In plain words Background work (like preparing a video) is a job with a strict life story and a budget. A job can retry, fall back, or fail — but it can never hang forever, and whatever it shows the user is always an honest state, never an endless spinner.

User-visible states are QUEUED · PREPARING · REVIEWING · AVAILABLE · AVAILABLE_WITH_FALLBACK · NEEDS_REVIEW · FAILED · CANCELLED.

Events glue the two worlds together

Everything that matters emits an event — source.ingested, claim.published, claim.stale (which also cancels or parks in-flight asset jobs bound to that claim), asset.published, answer.delivered, user_data.delete_requested, and so on. Event rules: at-least-once delivery, idempotent consumers, immutable event IDs, tenant + trace context, schema versions, bounded retries with dead-lettering, and no raw user media in payloads.

Notifications don’t get special trust

  • The payload references the answer/job — it never embeds licensed source content.
  • Opening a notification starts a fresh retrieval and Delivery Gate check; the historic payload is never trusted as current truth.
  • Notification failure never changes publication state; duplicates collapse on an idempotency key.

Remembering the conversation

Maps to LLD §5.15 — the Session Service and entity bindings.

In plain words When you say “what about the other latch?”, the system doesn’t vaguely guess from chat history. The session keeps a typed stack of what “this”, “that latch”, and “there” currently point to — real entity IDs bound to the product graph. If a reference could mean two things, it asks; it never picks one arbitrarily.

The session stores the active product/revision, user constraints, current answer and claim references, active procedure step, pending deferred jobs, and an entity-binding stack: each binding records the entity, where it came from (a tapped image region, a step, a prior answer), and its confidence. Resolution precedence: explicit user correction → direct UI selection → current procedure/visual binding → recency-scored conversational binding. “The other one” resolves only inside a typed candidate set with exactly one member left. Every resolution is recorded in the new Answer Manifest, and any follow-up that changes identity, state, or constraints invalidates affected prior results.

Events / outboxTransactional outbox → consumersOutbox table in Postgres + relay to SQS/SNSEventBridge, Kafka later
Media delivery (published serving copies)Signed URLs, revocable, fast startCloudFront over S3 with signed URLsAny CDN (Fastly, Cloudflare); Mux for per-title packaging/ABR
Why co-location is the design The publish transaction needs claims, assets, and outbox in one database; the Delivery Gate’s batch eligibility check becomes a single-snapshot query; and the vector index sitting beside the registry rows it points to makes “never authoritative” structurally cheap to enforce. Scale-up paths swap services without touching contracts.
The publish rule Being in object storage or a search index makes nothing user-visible. Eligibility comes from the authoritative registry’s lifecycle state — and the Delivery Gate re-checks it at the moment of delivery. Publication itself uses an immutable version + a transactional outbox: the publish transaction re-verifies every bound claim is still servable, writes the version and the asset.published event together, and indexers merely follow along.

Version pinning: every Answer Manifest pins claim versions, asset versions, the identity result, all policy versions (raw-request, per-claim), planner/validator versions, calculation versions, the IR schema version, gate and realization-policy versions, and the delivery timestamp. Given a manifest, you can reconstruct exactly why the system said what it said.

Safety, rights, and privacy

Maps to LLD §12.

In plain words Three separate protections: security (nobody touches what they shouldn’t), rights (we only use content the way its owner allowed), and privacy (your uploads are yours — kept minimal, deletable everywhere).

Rights travel with content

Every source and asset carries a rights vector — may we store it, transform it, send it to a provider, generate from it, display it publicly, until when, with attribution? When an asset combines sources, its effective rights are the intersection of all source rights plus any derivative restriction. The Delivery Gate checks rights per display context at delivery time.

Security boundaries

  • Everything authenticated and authorized; tenant ID on every data-access path.
  • Parsers and media decoders run sandboxed; source content is data, never planner instructions (prompt-injection defense at the boundary).
  • Remote URL fetches are allow-listed and SSRF-protected; provider responses are untrusted candidate data.
  • Secrets never live in job payloads or logs; delivery uses expiring signed URLs where required.
  • Audit logs cover publishing, approvals, gate decisions, rights denials, and deletion.

Privacy

  • User media is stored apart from catalog data, with explicit minimal retention; it is never reused to build catalog assets without separate consent.
  • Every derivative remembers its user-data root ID, so deletion can chase it through objects, indexes, caches, sessions, logs, manifests, and review artifacts (subject to legal retention) — ending in a written deletion receipt.
  • Evaluation datasets require an explicit inclusion policy and de-identification.

All failures emit structured reason codes with trace context. Observability (LLD §14) ties it together: shared IDs (trace_id, answer_id, claim/asset versions, policy versions) across online and offline work; metrics on latency by stage, gate pass/repair/fail rates, retrieval eligibility rejections, verification pass rates, cost per published asset, invalidation propagation time; and structured logs with reason codes — never raw user media, never hidden chain-of-thought.

Proving it works

Maps to LLD §16 — unit, contract, golden-scenario, and end-to-end tests, plus release gates.

In plain words The safety rules aren’t vibes — they’re executable. Every “never” in this document has a test that tries to make the system do it anyway, and the release is blocked until the system refuses correctly.

Golden scenarios (a taste of the ~30 in §16.3)

  • A wrong-SKU asset is rejected despite high semantic similarity.
  • A generated candidate can never be retrieved; a stale asset is rejected after retrieval but before delivery.
  • A transition clip is never accepted as an instruction; realization can’t invert a typed conclusion.
  • A planner omission is caught by the completeness validator — and the semantic layer cannot waive a deterministic failure.
  • An exhausted video budget produces a verified illustrated fallback and an honest terminal status.
  • “The other latch”, when ambiguous, causes a clarification instead of an arbitrary pick.
  • A C3 request gets the deterministic floor and human-approved content only.
  • User-data deletion reaches every derivative; every delivered video has its accessible alternative.

Release gates

  • No P0/C3 production answer without required human approval.
  • Zero wrong-SKU results in the release identity/retrieval suite.
  • Zero delivery of candidate, stale, or retired assets; zero unbound product claims in realized responses.
  • All golden scenarios pass; security and privacy checks pass for enabled input modes.

The future: “will it fit?” done right

Maps to LLD §18 — the geometry extension boundary.

In plain words Today, if you ask “will this stroller fit in my trunk?”, ShowMe says exact fit isn’t available yet — because answering honestly requires real certified measurements of both the product and your trunk, not a good-looking 3D model. The plan: measurements go in, a certified representation (checked against reality, with a declared capability envelope) is published like any other claim, a deterministic engine — not an AI guess — computes fit/clearance/paths within that envelope, uncertainty is propagated, and the result flows through the same Answer IR and Delivery Gate as everything else. The 3D rendering only communicates the conclusion; it never establishes it.

Key boundary: certification is capability-specific. A model certified for external dimensions is not thereby valid for collision, folding kinematics, required force, stability, or electrical behavior. And exact fit needs the user’s environment too: openings, orientation, maneuver path, closure state, agreed tolerance. Until all of that exists, the geometry route stays a typed extension point (reserved in the route table), and the honest answer is “not yet”.
