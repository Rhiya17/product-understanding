7. REFINE THE LARGE FORMS FIRST

Work in a few focused passes. Choose techniques based on the evidence; do not force every component into one primitive or modifier recipe.

Before changing soft-part topology, inspect its deformation contract. The current soft_grid meshes depend on soft_kind, nu/nv, vertex ordering, and rig.py's generated coordinates/shape keys. Rigid parts use parent transforms and parent-inverse matrices, while several soft surfaces receive coordinates already transformed into world space. Reparenting those surfaces without converting coordinates can apply motion twice.

You may replace the simplified fabric construction. If topology changes, regenerate compatible Basis geometry, all affected shape keys/deformation data, UVs, and attached details together. Verify vertex counts and correspondence; never assume old keys fit a new mesh. Check modifier order, rest state, object scale, and thickness before rebaking. Preserve logical part identity without preserving a poor mesh recipe. Do not blindly apply transforms to animated objects.

Canopy:
- Match the crown, front overhang/visor, side profile, panel divisions, visible bows, and attachment locations.
- Give panels convincing curvature and transitions rather than one uniformly smooth dome.
- Preserve distinct extended and gathered states, connected by coherent deformation.
- Avoid unexplained rear flaps, sharp corrugations, detached edges, or fabric that passes through the handle.

Seat and backrest:
- Match recline, width, depth, calf-support profile, and the relationship to frame and belly bar.
- Model the visible thickness and compression of upholstery, rounded sewn edges, and separate side wings.
- Make padding look supported by the underlying seat structure. Avoid an inflated slab or a thin sheet with a uniformly rounded edge.
- Keep harness straps, pads, and buckle positioned consistently with the seat in every pose.

Basket:
- Match the opening, sag, side shape, edge binding, and attachment locations.
- Make the mesh and solid fabric regions visually legible.
- The folded basket should gather plausibly around its attachments instead of remaining a rigid tray.

Frame, wheels, grip, and molded parts:
- Refine observable tube paths and cross sections, transition pieces, side hubs, forks, wheel/spoke forms, and tread/rim proportions. Verify the actual rim and accent shapes rather than elaborating the baseline's decorative silver loops.
- Improve seams between assembled parts, supported fasteners, grip shape, and the control housing where references support them.
- Preserve separate moving assemblies and stable part identifiers. If a component is replaced during construction, document its mapping to the previous identifier.
- Avoid speculative mechanical detail on unseen surfaces.

After each substantive geometry change, inspect open, middle, late if relevant, and folded poses. Correct new collisions, detached details, or envelope changes before investing in surface detail.

Geometry gate: save neutral-material comparisons that show the priority silhouette/volume corrections in compatible reference states, plus coherent side/rear and intermediate-fold views. The canopy must read as connected panels, the seat as supported upholstery, and the basket as suspended fabric. Resolve major new intersections or detachments before final surface polish. If evidence is insufficient or a correction fails, retain the best checkpoint and record the limitation.

8. ADD FABRIC CONSTRUCTION AND MATERIAL RESPONSE

Fabric construction:
- Add visible seams, piping, hems, panel boundaries, and attachment-driven folds at appropriate scale.
- Attach these details to the deforming surfaces so they remain aligned during folding.
- Use coherent fabric orientation/UVs or another validated mapping method, with a plausible physical weave scale across panels. Check stretching, sliding, and scale changes through the fold. The basket currently uses Generated coordinates: these are based on undeformed positions, so do not assume they inherently slide during animation. Their per-object normalization can still produce inconsistent physical pattern scale; inspect the actual result and replace the mapping only where necessary.
- Prioritize broad folds and compression before tiny wrinkles or individual stitch detail.
- Avoid decorative wrinkles unrelated to supports, gravity, or compression.

Materials:
- Upholstery/canopy: plausible woven structure, subtle color variation, appropriate roughness and restrained fiber sheen. Generic noise is not sufficient evidence of a woven appearance.
- Grip: reference-supported tan color, fine grain and seam detail, with plausible roughness.
- Tires: matte rubber with controlled tread detail and subdued reflection.
- Molded plastic: distinguish its finish from rubber and frame surfaces.
- Frame and wheel accents: use plausible finishes guided by the photographs; do not label a material as a specific alloy/coating unless supported.

Use physically plausible shader values as hypotheses; photos do not establish exact roughness, fiber type, alloy, or other physical properties. Inspect close-ups under neutral lighting and at final display scale. The basket must retain visible mesh/solid regions without disappearing, looking like an opaque sheet, or flickering. Fine detail should remain subtle; avoid excessive bump, moire, glossy cloth, and denoising that erases the weave. Use geometry for silhouette-changing hems/padding and shading for detail too small to justify geometry.

Prefer procedural textures or already permitted local materials. If texture images are used, pack them or use portable relative paths, and record their source. Do not use a projected product photograph as a substitute for coherent geometry across views.

Keep geometry, material, and lighting improvements distinguishable in the comparison record. Do not obscure remaining shape differences with dark lighting, blur, or shallow depth of field.

Material gate: under the same neutral lighting, cloth, grip, tire, molded plastic, and frame must be visually distinguishable, with stable surface detail in open and folded states. A glossy hero image alone does not satisfy this gate.

9. IMPROVE VISIBLE FABRIC MOTION WITHOUT EXPANDING THE MECHANICS SCOPE

The existing kinematic action is a useful starting point. Preserve the release-before-collapse sequence and inspect visible trajectories against the original timestamps. Change the rig only when evidence or a visible defect justifies it, and document the reason.

For soft parts, choose a practical approach:
- Refined shape keys/deformation controls are acceptable when they give stable, credible motion.
- Cloth simulation is optional. Allow one bounded feasibility test on a single canopy or basket region, after its geometry and attachments are correct. Define pins, rest shape, collision proxies, thickness, and supported cache/export behavior. Inspect a representative transition before expanding its use.
- Invalidate and rebake affected caches after mesh, pin, collision, rest-state, or timing changes. Keep required final caches inside the output run, with portable paths and frame ranges; temporary caches must not be the final scene's only dependency.
- Reject the simulation if it introduces popping, penetration, unstable thickness, detached pins, unreproducible caches, or impractical render/bake cost. Preserve the best stable deformation and label it INFERRED. Do not turn this pass into open-ended cloth troubleshooting.

Full rigid-body dynamics, mass estimation, spring/cable reconstruction, and force validation are outside this visual-realism pass.

Throughout the action:
- Rigid members retain constant dimensions.
- The same model persists; do not hide parts, switch models, or replace entire silhouettes by pose.
- Padding and fabric may deform, but attachments and thickness must remain plausible.
- Seams and material patterns follow their surfaces.
- Wheels, feet/supports, and the floor have coherent contact relationships.

Keep that mapping unless there is a specific reason to revise it. Record any new mapping once for the whole shot; do not choose different source times for different parts.

If using simulated cloth for the continuous fold, drive the rigid motion at source speed, bake a supported representation, then retime cloth and rigid parts together for presentation. Simulating directly over a slowed timeline changes gravity/inertia behavior and is not equivalent. Preparation timing remains illustrative where the source has separate shots. If consistent retiming cannot be reproduced with the available tools, prefer controlled deformation over an unverified simulation claim.

Keep the existing procedure coverage: empty stroller, brakes assumed unlocked, canopy preparation, cup/wheel tip treatment, thumb switch before squeeze lever, frame collapse, and source-backed secure/carry panels.
